# One-Minute Video Production Guide

Use this guide to build the private plan and review the output. Adapt the structure to the user's genre rather than forcing every project into the same pattern.

## Continuity bible

Record only details that can be observed or that materially affect generation.

### Format
- Exact target: 60 seconds
- Aspect ratio, resolution, and frame-rate feel
- Platform and safe areas
- Live action, animation, game footage, product render, or another medium

### Subjects
- Identity anchors: face, age, build, hair, skin/fur/material, and distinctive traits
- Wardrobe and accessories
- Hero props, products, vehicles, and exact colors
- Allowed emotional and physical progression

### World
- Location and spatial layout
- Time of day, weather, atmosphere, and particles
- Light direction, hardness, exposure, shadows, palette, and color grade
- Background landmarks that must persist

### Camera and style
- Shot language and lens feel
- Camera height, movement, stabilization, focus, and motion blur
- Texture, contrast, sharpness, grain, bloom, and rendering style
- Transition language: continuous movement, match cut, hard cut, dissolve, or another deliberate choice

### Audio
- Spoken language and voice profile when requested
- Music mood and intensity curve
- Ambience and recurring sound motifs
- Moments that need effects or silence

## Timeline patterns

Every timeline must total exactly 60 seconds. These are starting points.

### Story or cinematic short
- 0–3: visual hook
- 3–12: establish subject, world, and goal
- 12–27: rising action
- 27–43: complication or reveal
- 43–55: climax and resolution
- 55–60: memorable closing image

### Product or service ad
- 0–3: hook or pain point
- 3–12: situation and audience need
- 12–27: product reveal and primary benefit
- 27–43: demonstration and proof that is supported by the brief
- 43–54: secondary benefit and outcome
- 54–60: brand-safe closing and call to action

### Explainer
- 0–4: question or promise
- 4–14: context
- 14–38: three clear points or steps
- 38–51: example or result
- 51–57: concise recap
- 57–60: final takeaway

### Social reel
- 0–2: immediate motion or statement
- 2–10: setup
- 10–42: fast, readable progression in a few beats
- 42–54: payoff
- 54–60: loopable or memorable ending

## Segment planning

- Prefer two 30-second segments when the model reliably supports 30 seconds and can use reference video or frames.
- Use shorter segments for complex action, deliberate edits, or when the model's consistency falls over longer generations.
- Keep generated material slightly longer than required only when trimming handles will not damage motion or speech.
- For seamless joins, choose a stable but active handoff frame: clear identity, readable pose, low occlusion, and a simple motion vector.
- Avoid joining during rapid blur, flashes, dense particles, face occlusion, or geometry changes unless the transition is intentional.

## Review checkpoints

### Visual
- Hook is clear in the first 3 seconds.
- Subject identity and product geometry remain stable.
- Screen direction and eye lines are intentional.
- Camera language and color treatment feel like one production.
- No accidental text, logo, watermark, morph, extra limb, or background mutation appears.

### Editorial
- Scene durations support comprehension.
- The same action is not repeated across a join.
- Transitions motivate changes in place, time, or angle.
- The ending resolves the central promise and holds long enough to read.

### Audio
- Voice is intelligible and synchronized.
- Music supports rather than masks speech.
- Ambience does not jump between connected shots.
- Effects land on visible events.
- Peaks do not clip and the ending does not cut off abruptly.
