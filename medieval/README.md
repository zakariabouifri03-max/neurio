# IRONVOW — a duel of steel and wind

A first-person medieval melee game. Not a shooter with swords: every cut is a
real swing through space, every point of contact between steel and a body or
between two blades is resolved as physics, and the thing that decides a bout is
whether you put your steel where his edge was going.

Everything in it — the men, the harness, the weapons, the halls, the textures,
the sounds — is generated by code in this folder. There is no downloaded art, no
fonts, no CDN, no network access of any kind. Three.js r170 is vendored in
`vendor/` and mapped by an import map in `index.html`.

## Run it

```bash
cd medieval
python3 -m http.server 8000
# open http://localhost:8000/
```

Any static server will do; it must be served over http(s) (or localhost), not
`file://`, because it is ES modules. `test/smoke.html` is a two-minute page that
draws a rig and a weapon in raw WebGL if you want to check a browser's 3D before
playing.

## Controls

| | |
|---|---|
| mouse | look — **your body faces where you look**, and so does your blade |
| **LMB** | wind a blow; **hold** to charge it, **release** to commit it. The line comes from the mouse motion: pull it left, or up, and the cut goes there (`I J K L` / numpad force a line) |
| **RMB** | raise the guard, and hold it. The guard follows your eyes: look left and you cover left |
| **Q** | swap which side the point is on &nbsp;•&nbsp; **R** cycles the stance guard |
| **E** | feint — break off a wind-up midway (a real one: it costs wind and buys nothing if he reads it) |
| **Space** | shove (a body blow with the shield or a gauntlet: opens distance, no damage) |
| **WASD** / **Shift** | step, retreat, sprint |
| **Tab** | lock on &nbsp;•&nbsp; **F** flash the blade &nbsp;•&nbsp; **Y** yield &nbsp;•&nbsp; **Esc/P** pause |

Press **Enter** on the boot card, then pick a mode on the boards: a drill from
the ladder, quick play, or the armoury (weapon, harness, shield — the choice
decides whether the fight is a fencing match or a wrestling bout in plate).

## What the simulation actually does

**Steel.** A weapon is a set of capsules with radii, carried by a transform that
is sampled every frame. A blow is resolved by sweeping each capsule through its
own motion during the substep and asking what it met first — a body region,
another man's weapon, a shield rim, the floor. Contact energy is the effective
mass *along the blade* at that fraction of the weapon times the closing speed:
a cut with the last third of the blade is a different animal from the same blow
landed with the strong. A thrust runs down the axis and concentrates; a flat
claps and bruises.

**Armour.** Every body region has a channel — cut, blunt, pierce — and each
harness layer (gambeson, mail, leather, plate) absorbs differently in each
channel, with coverage and gaps per region: a throat, an armpit, a knee, an
open visor. Damage that gets through accumulates as bleeding and as disabled
regions, and a disabled arm or leg changes what a man can do rather than just
subtracting hit points.

**The guard.** A guard is not a wall of numbers. The defender solves, every
frame, where the incoming steel will be a fraction of a second ahead (the
prediction is *rotational* — the frame turns and the steel follows the arc), and
lays his blade across that line. A raised guard has its hilt, gauntlet and
forearm in the way too, so it catches more than a blade lying in the grass. A
parry is the same solve inside a short window, and it gives the defender far
more slack — which is what makes a timed defence beat a held one. A blow that
arrives with enough energy behind it, or against a man whose wind is gone,
beats the guard open and carries on into him at a discount.

**Wind.** Stamina drains while you hold a guard, swing, sprint or take a beating
on the arms, and comes back slowly — and not at all while you stand there with
your blade up. A man who fights behind his own guard all day is a man who cannot
raise a blow.

**Reactions.** Hits land as an impulse with a direction: heads snap, arms fly
out, a cut to the thigh folds the leg and the man drops. A rattled man staggers
with his guard worthless, can't be struck by another man *while* he is
staggering off the first one, and — if he is sensible, or losing badly — can ask
for quarter: he drops his point and the hall takes his name off the round.
Bouts are decided by death, by quarter, or by the marshal counting blows when
the pass runs long.

## Files

```
index.html            the shell: boot card, HUD/menu mount, import map, service worker
sw.js                 offline cache for the whole game
manifest.webmanifest  installable app metadata; icons/ are hand-written SVG
src/main.js           Game: boot, bouts, rounds, the director, input→intent
src/fighter.js        Fighter: the shared body — pose, wind, armour, state machine, guard
src/defense.js        threatPoint / solveGuard: where the blow will be, where steel must go
src/exchange.js       one frame of a meeting: steel vs steel, steel vs flesh, binds
src/combat.js         WeaponBody, sweeps, contact → damage channels, BodyState
src/characters.js     Rig: procedural bodies, bones, layered armour, weak points
src/weapons.js        the smithy: every weapon and shield is built here
src/moves.js          authored poses and movesets (every blow is a real path)
src/ai.js             Brain: reads, spacing, aggression, feints, yields
src/arena.js          the halls: stone, timber, torches, fog, bounds, colliders
src/tex.js            procedural canvas textures and materials
src/vfx.js audio.js   sparks, blood, dust, blade trail; synthesized sound
src/hud.js menu.js state.js input.js view.js tuning.js data.js mathx.js
```

`tuning.js` holds every constant worth arguing about and `data.js` holds all the
content: houses, weapons, harness, champions, drills, ranks.

## Tests and tools

```bash
node tools/logic-test.mjs     # the full rules harness — physics, armour, guard, AI, saves
node tools/shell-test.mjs     # the shell: menus, boards, a whole bout through main.js
node dbg19.mjs                # balance sweep: two men of equal skill, five dials, per-seed ends
node dbg33.mjs                # head-to-head: does skill actually win fights?
node dbg22.mjs                # the reach table: what each weapon and blow can actually touch
node dbg25.mjs                # the guard matrix: each attack line against each guard
node dbg26.mjs dbg27.mjs      # a blow-by-blow trace of one duel, with the guard solver's output
node dbg13.mjs dbg14.mjs dbg15.mjs   # damage verification for a known blow
```

The harnesses resolve the bare specifier `three` through a small local shim,
which is not committed (the browser never needs it — it uses `vendor/`):

```bash
cd medieval && mkdir -p node_modules/three && cp vendor/three.module.js node_modules/three/ \
  && printf '{"name":"three","version":"0.170.0","type":"module","main":"three.module.js"}' > node_modules/three/package.json
```

Both harnesses run headless in Node against `tools/domstub.mjs` (a DOM and
canvas stub — no WebGL, so they drive the simulation directly). They are the
reason a 60-second duel can be checked in two seconds.
