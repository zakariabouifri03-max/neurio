# Research ledger — "Lock the Clock" (1974 permanent-DST experiment)

Every number used on screen is sourced here. Sunrise times were *computed* with a
NOAA sunrise equation (±1 min) rather than quoted, so the map matches the record.

## The law
- Nixon signed the **Emergency Daylight Saving Time Energy Conservation Act**
  (Pub. L. 93–182) on **Dec 15, 1973**, making DST year-round as a **two-year trial**.
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_Daylight_Saving_Time_Energy_Conservation_Act), [Politifact](https://politifact.com/factchecks/2026/jul/20/social-media/a-1973-law-made-daylight-saving-yearlong-for-a-trial-period-then-it-was-canceled-early/)
- Context: **1973 OPEC oil embargo**, gas lines, 55 mph limit. Nixon claimed it would
  save an estimated **150,000 barrels of oil/day** and bring "only a minimum of
  inconvenience." [History.com](https://www.history.com/articles/permanent-daylight-saving-time-1970s)
- It began **Jan 6, 1974** (clocks sprang forward at 2 a.m. in mid-winter).

## The dark mornings (computed sunrises, Jan 6, permanent DST)
- Seattle **8:56 a.m.**, Minneapolis **8:50 a.m.**, **Detroit 9:00 a.m.**,
  Chicago 8:17 a.m., Fargo **9:11 a.m.**, New York 8:19 a.m., Miami 8:08 a.m.
  (computed with NOAA sunrise equation). Matches reporting that sunrise hit
  "around 9 a.m." in Detroit/Minneapolis/Seattle. [History.com](https://www.history.com/articles/permanent-daylight-saving-time-1970s)

## The human cost
- In Florida, **8 schoolchildren were killed** on morning commutes in the first weeks
  of Jan 1974 vs. **2 the year before**. Parents and safety advocates dubbed it
  "**Daylight Disaster Time**." Causation was disputed by some state officials.
  [Tallahassee / USA Today](https://www.tallahassee.com/story/news/politics/2026/07/15/permanent-daylight-saving-time-failed/90926351007/), [PNJ](https://www.pnj.com/story/news/state/2026/09/25/florida-fought-over-repealing-daylight-saving-time/91920574007/)

## Public opinion
- NORC (U. of Chicago) poll: support **79% in Dec 1973 → 42% by Feb 1974**.
  Primary opposition: children going to school in the dark.
  [AASM fact sheet](https://aasm.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/sunshine-protection-act-fact-sheet-daylight-saving-time.pdf), [Politifact](https://politifact.com/factchecks/2026/jul/20/social-media/a-1973-law-made-daylight-saving-yearlong-for-a-trial-period-then-it-was-canceled-early/)

## The energy verdict
- DOT (1975) claimed ~**1%** electricity savings; the National Bureau of Standards
  (1976) review found that **not statistically significant**.
  [CEPR VoxEU](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/does-daylight-saving-time-save-electricity), [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/does-daylight-saving-times-save-energy/)

## The reversal
- Congress repealed it; Americans rolled clocks back **Oct 27, 1974** (≈9.5 months in).
  [Politifact](https://politifact.com/factchecks/2026/jul/20/social-media/a-1973-law-made-daylight-saving-yearlong-for-a-trial-period-then-it-was-canceled-early/), [Mercury News](https://www.mercurynews.com/2022/03/25/editorial-we-tried-year-round-daylight-saving-time-in-1974-people-hated-it/)

## Why it matters now (2026)
- The House passed the **Sunshine Protection Act 308–117 on July 14, 2026**; the Senate
  has not voted; next clock change **Nov 1, 2026**. The same experiment is on the table.
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunshine_Protection_Act), [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/nation/2026/07/16/permanent-daylight-saving-time-past-efforts/90941432007/)
