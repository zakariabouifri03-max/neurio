## 1. The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower. Twice.

- **Title:** The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower. Twice.
- **Thumbnail text:** "SOLD TWICE"
- **Hook:** "In 1925, one man sold the Eiffel Tower for scrap. Then he did it again."
- **Payoff:** The tower was never for sale; the first buyer was too ashamed to call the police — and that silence, not the con, is the real lesson about how fraud survives.
- **Why it performs:** Con-artist stories are evergreen, hugely rewatchable and clips well at every act break. Series potential: "Great Impostors", one 10-min episode per con.
- **Best length:** 10 min
- **Fact status:** Verified. 1925, Victor Lustig posed as a senior official of the Ministry of Posts and Telegraphs, forged letterhead, met scrap dealers at the Hôtel de Crillon; André Poisson paid ~70,000 francs and never reported it. **Nuance to state on screen:** the second attempt is documented, but the Eiffel Tower's own history says the second buyer grew suspicious before the deal closed — so "sold twice" is the legend, one completed sale plus a documented second attempt is the record.

## 2. 33,000 People, Six Acres, No Government

- **Title:** 33,000 People, Six Acres, No Government
- **Thumbnail text:** "DENSEST PLACE ON EARTH"
- **Hook:** "For forty years, one city block in Hong Kong held more people per square metre than anywhere in recorded history — and nobody governed it."
- **Payoff:** Kowloon Walled City was not chaos. With no authority above it, residents built their own dentists, doctors, bakeries and schools. The lesson is about order without a state.
- **Why it performs:** Density visuals are a thumbnail cheat code, and "lawless place that actually worked" is a proven watch-time engine. Series: "Places That Shouldn't Exist."
- **Best length:** 10 min
- **Fact status:** Verified. ~33,000 residents per the 1987 survey, ~2.6 hectares (6.4 acres), ~300 interconnected buildings up to 14 storeys, cleared 1993–94, site now Kowloon Walled City Park (opened 1995). Population estimates vary 33,000–50,000 — say "roughly 33,000" on screen.

## 3. The Bubble That Never Happened

- **Title:** The Bubble That Never Happened
- **Thumbnail text:** "TULIP MANIA WAS A LIE"
- **Hook:** "You've been told a story about tulips, greed and ruin for three hundred years. The archives say it mostly didn't happen."
- **Payoff:** The mania was real but small — a few hundred wealthy traders, almost no bankruptcies — and most of the "everyone went broke" detail came from satirical pamphlets written after the crash.
- **Why it performs:** Contrarian myth-busting gets the highest comment volume of any money format, and every crypto cycle revives it. Series: "Money Myths That Won't Die", one per episode.
- **Best length:** 5 min
- **Fact status:** Verified. Historian Anne Goldgar's archival work (*Tulipmania*, 2007) found only 37 buyers who spent more than 300 guilders (about a craftsman's yearly wage), traced no bankruptcies to tulips, and traced the myth to satirical pamphlets via Charles Mackay. Rare bulbs did reach extraordinary prices (Garber's tables: Semper Augustus ~6,290 guilders on 5 Feb 1637) — both halves must be in the script or it becomes its own myth.

## 4. The Company That Invented Tomorrow and Buried It

- **Title:** The Company That Invented Tomorrow and Buried It
- **Thumbnail text:** "THEY INVENTED IT. IT KILLED THEM."
- **Hook:** "In 1975 a 24-year-old built the first digital camera. His employer told him to keep it quiet. His employer was Kodak."
- **Payoff:** Kodak didn't miss the future — it saw the future, priced it against its own film revenue, and chose the revenue. The mechanism, not the blindness, is the story.
- **Why it performs:** Business-disruption stories are the strongest monetisation niche for faceless channels and are endlessly remade. Series: "The Company That Saw It Coming" (Blockbuster, Nokia, Xerox PARC).
- **Best length:** 10 min
- **Fact status:** Verified. Dec 1975, Steve Sasson, ~8 lb prototype, 100×100 px (0.01 MP), 23 s per image to cassette, patent US 4,131,919. **Careful:** the popular version ("they buried it and went bankrupt because of it") is too simple — Kodak patented heavily, invested in digital, and for a period sold more consumer digital cameras in the US than anyone. Script the real failure (protecting film margin) or the video gets debunked in the comments.

## 5. The Math That Was Impossible for Seven Months

- **Title:** The Math That Was Impossible for Seven Months
- **Thumbnail text:** "50% IN 45 DAYS"
- **Hook:** "In 1920, a man in Boston promised fifty percent profit in forty-five days. Forty thousand people believed him. The arithmetic never worked."
- **Payoff:** Ponzi's own story is the boring part; the interesting part is that the scheme was *plausible by design* — 50% sounds like a clever arbitrage, not a fantasy.
- **Why it performs:** Fraud is the most durable faceless format, and the "how to spot it" ending gives a shareable payoff. Series: "How the Scheme Worked", one per fraud.
- **Best length:** 10 min
- **Fact status:** Verified. Securities Exchange Company, ~$15M from ~40,000 investors in 1920, 50% / 45 days, ~27,000 reply coupons existed worldwide versus the ~160 million that would have been needed, ~$200 left at collapse, collapsed August 1920. Figures vary slightly between sources; pick one and caption it.

## 6. Your Brain Would Rather Be Right Than Rich

- **Title:** Your Brain Would Rather Be Right Than Rich
- **Thumbnail text:** "BEING RIGHT IS EXPENSIVE"
- **Hook:** "You don't protect your money. You protect your identity — and your money pays for it."
- **Payoff:** Sunk cost, loss aversion and commitment bias, told as four ordinary decisions (a bad investment, a dead-end job, an argument, a subscription) and the one question that breaks each.
- **Why it performs:** Psychology explainers with a money frame hit both the self-improvement and finance audiences. Series: "The Expensive Brain", one bias per episode.
- **Best length:** 5 min
- **Fact status:** No statistics required — keep it on mechanisms and named effects, and avoid attaching invented numbers to studies. If you want a study cited (Kahneman & Tversky, Arkes & Blumer), it must be checked at scripting time.

## 7. Why the Snack Aisle Makes More Money Than the Jewelry Counter

- **Title:** Why the Snack Aisle Makes More Money Than the Jewelry Counter
- **Thumbnail text:** "CHEAP BEATS LUXURY"
- **Hook:** "The most profitable thing in the supermarket costs about a dollar and is designed to be bought a hundred times."
- **Payoff:** Margin per unit is a vanity number; margin per customer per year is the business. Cheap-and-repeated beats expensive-and-rare, and the shop knows it before you walk in.
- **Why it performs:** Retail psychology is a strong evergreen with high retention on visuals (shelves, pricing tags, counters). Series: "Why the Shop Is Designed Like This."
- **Best length:** 5 min
- **Fact status:** **Needs checking before scripting.** Any real company margin figures or category profit numbers must be sourced; build the episode on mechanism (frequency × margin, shelf placement, loss leaders) if verification is thin.

## 8. The $200 Machine That Was "Cute"

- **Title:** The $200 Machine That Was "Cute"
- **Thumbnail text:** "CUTE. DON'T TELL ANYONE."
- **Hook:** "The most important machine of the last fifty years was called 'cute' in the room where it was shown."
- **Payoff:** Every industry has one object that seems like a toy until it isn't. The tell is not quality — it's who owns the margin it threatens.
- **Why it performs:** Short-form-length story with one perfect line ("don't tell anyone") that clips well and seeds the longer episode. Series: "First Versions", one per invention.
- **Best length:** 3 min
- **Fact status:** Verified (same evidence as idea #4: Sasson, Dec 1975, 0.01 MP, 23 s, cassette). Do not claim Kodak refused to develop digital at all — see the caveat in #4. Consider merging with #4 if you want one 10-min business episode instead of two.

## 9. Motivation Is a Liar

- **Title:** Motivation Is a Liar
- **Thumbnail text:** "MOTIVATION IS A LIAR"
- **Hook:** "You don't need more motivation. You need a smaller first step and a shorter memory for failure."
- **Payoff:** Five mechanics that beat willpower — shrinking the entry cost, pre-committing, removing the decision, tracking the streak, and designing the environment — each shown as a small scene with a rubber stamp landing on it.
- **Why it performs:** The single best-performing evergreen for faceless channels, and the format rewards the skill's hand-lettered labels and stamps. Series: "The Small Mechanics", one habit per episode.
- **Best length:** 5 min
- **Fact status:** Advice, not claims. Keep neuroscience general ("dopamine is about anticipation, not reward") and don't cite a percentage or a study unless verified.

## 10. The Prison Experiment That Wasn't

- **Title:** The Prison Experiment That Wasn't
- **Thumbnail text:** "THE STUDY WAS FAKE"
- **Hook:** "The most famous experiment in psychology was taught in every school for fifty years. Then someone read the tapes."
- **Payoff:** The guards were coached, the results were selected, and the story survived because it flattered what people already believed about power.
- **Why it performs:** Myth-busting plus psychology is a double-retention format, and the "you were taught this" hook drives comments. Series: "You Were Taught This."
- **Best length:** 10 min
- **Fact status:** **Needs checking before scripting.** The Stanford Prison Experiment is genuinely heavily criticised (released audio, Ben Blum's reporting, Le Texier's 2019 analysis), but every specific claim — dates, the coaching quote, the 1971 timeline — must be verified from primary sources before it goes in the script.

## Recommended order

1. **#4 or #5** — strongest niche, fully verified, and the visuals (documents, counters, charts) match the skill's shot library exactly.
2. **#2** — the thumbnail does the work; density is easy to draw and hard to scroll past.
3. **#3** — cheapest to make honestly (5 min), highest comment bait.
4. **#1** — best hook line of the ten; save it for when the pipeline is smooth.

**Fact discipline:** ideas 7 and 10 are the only ones I could not verify to scripting standard. Everything else above is sourced, with the two caveats (Eiffel Tower second sale, Kodak's digital history) written into the idea so the script doesn't repeat the popular version.
